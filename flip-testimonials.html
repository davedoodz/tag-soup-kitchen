Flip-card testimonials — 5 explorations

Flip mechanics lifted measurement-for-measurement from davidlafond.xyz: 430×430 cards, 38px radius, perspective:1200px on the card, preserve-3d flipper transitioning transform .62s cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1), absolutely-positioned faces with backface-visibility:hidden, back face at rotateY(180deg), on the #030303 / #0B0B0C / #F5F2EA palette in Manrope. Every card carries real text on both faces. Quotes are placeholder copy, not your clients' actual words. Cards are real <button>s: click, or Tab + Enter/Space. The hidden face is inert so screen readers never read both sides at once, and prefers-reduced-motion swaps the 3D rotation for a cross-fade.

E1 Band — faithful copy Your existing pattern: a clipped viewport over a wide track drifting right to left, cards flipping in place. Drift pauses on hover and on keyboard focus, so a flipped card can actually be read.

E2 Quad — light front, dark back Four static cards, no drift. The flip also inverts the surface: paper front for scanning, your #0B0B0C card on the back for the detail. The tonal switch does the explaining, so no "flip me" affordance is needed.
E3 Rows — vertical flip Same mechanic on the X axis so it reads as a page turning rather than a card spinning. Front is the one-line claim, back is the number behind it. Fits many voices in little height.
